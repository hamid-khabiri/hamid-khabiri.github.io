3-DOF Upper-Limb Rehabilitation Robotic System

Affiliation: University of Alberta, Canada (Postdoctoral Fellow – Robotics R&D, 2024 – present)

A custom 3-DOF planar robotic system for upper-limb rehabilitation that I independently conceived, designed and built: from requirements and mechanism architecture through fabrication, assembly, electrical integration, real-time control and testing.

Project overview

Upper-limb rehabilitation after neurological injury requires many repetitions of guided, controlled movement. This robot is designed to move or assist the user’s arm and forearm/wrist while remaining safe, testable and manufacturable.

The system combines a capstan-based rotary transmission, a differential timing-belt mechanism and a forearm/wrist end-effector, driven by three Maxon BLDC motors with EPOS4 drives. Real-time control runs in Beckhoff TwinCAT 3 over EtherCAT, with a TwinCAT HMI and Unity rehabilitation games connected through ADS.

My contribution

  • Independently conceived, designed and built the complete system, from requirements and mechanism architecture through fabrication, assembly, electrical integration and testing.
  • Designed the capstan-based rotary transmission, differential timing-belt mechanism and forearm/wrist end-effector in SolidWorks; completed calculations, tolerances, material selection, DFM and CNC manufacturing drawings.
  • Selected, assembled and integrated three Maxon BLDC motors with EPOS4 EtherCAT drives, transmissions, absolute encoders and force/torque sensing; wired the electrical hardware and calibrated sensor feedback.
  • Developed real-time, multi-axis motion control in TwinCAT 3 (Structured Text, PLC/NC) over EtherCAT; configured PDO/SDO mapping and position/velocity/torque modes, and diagnosed fieldbus faults.
  • Implemented cascaded control: tuned the EPOS4 inner current, velocity and position loops in EPOS Studio and developed outer-loop joint PID controllers in TwinCAT.
  • Implemented FK/IK, Jacobian-based control and trajectory planning; developed impedance control from joint position/velocity feedback and admittance control using force/torque sensor feedback.
  • Built a URDF dynamics model of the planar robot in Pinocchio, streamed to TwinCAT via ADS, and combined it with Coulomb–viscous friction modeling for model-based feedforward.
  • Developed a TwinCAT HMI and Unity rehabilitation games connected to live robot motion via ADS.

Control stack

  • Inner loops: EPOS4 current, velocity and position control (tuned in EPOS Studio)
  • Outer loops: joint PID controllers in TwinCAT 3
  • Impedance control from joint position/velocity feedback
  • Admittance control from force/torque sensor feedback
  • Model-based feedforward: Pinocchio rigid-body dynamics + Coulomb–viscous friction, via ADS
  • FK/IK, Jacobians and trajectory planning

Safety & verification

  • Hazard and risk assessment
  • Hardware layer: emergency stop, limit switches, Safe Torque Off on the drives
  • Software layer: virtual walls, HMI stop and fault handling
  • Structured bench verification and subsystem testing
  • Design iterated using measured actuator performance, fault investigation, preliminary usability observations with five participants and clinician feedback
