Effect of Eccentric Exercise on Isometric Contraction of Knee Extensor Muscles

Affiliation: Sahand University of Technology, Iran (MSc thesis, Mechanical Engineering – Biomechanics)

An experimental and modelling study of the effects of eccentric exercise and delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) on the knee extensor muscles. I executed the full project, from data acquisition to EMG and torque analysis and musculoskeletal modelling; the task design was set by the physiotherapist advisor.

BiomechanicsIsokinetic dynamometryEMGTorque analysisAnyBody modelling

Project overview

Fifteen untrained young male volunteers (mean age 26 years) performed eccentric quadriceps exercise on an isokinetic dynamometer at 120°/s over a knee range of 10° to 90°. The exercise was repeated 14 days later in the same way.

Maximum voluntary contraction during concentric contraction at 30 and 60°/s and explosive isometric contraction at 30°, 60°, 90° and 120° were measured before, immediately after, and 48 and 96 hours after both sessions, while changes in torque and EMG were tracked.

My contribution

  • Carried out all data acquisition with the isokinetic dynamometer and EMG.
  • Analysed EMG and torque data across all time points and sessions.
  • Built the musculoskeletal model in AnyBody and compared experimental results with the model.

Method

  • 15 participants, two exercise sessions 14 days apart
  • Isokinetic eccentric exercise at 120°/s
  • Concentric MVC and explosive isometric tests at multiple angles
  • EMG and torque measured at four time points
  • Musculoskeletal modelling in AnyBody

Findings

  • The largest reduction in MVC was predicted to occur 24 hours after exercise.
  • Maximum extension torque was predicted at about 86° of knee flexion.
  • Torque variability increased after eccentric exercise and with the onset of DOMS.
